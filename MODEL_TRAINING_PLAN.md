# Six Nations Picker — Model Training Plan

## Context
The extraction layer is **done and frozen** (see `DATA_EXTRACTION_PLAN.md`). Two PIT-clean,
reproducible stores join 1:1 on `(fixture_id, player_id)`:
- `data/model_player_match.csv` — **2,760 rows × 91 feature cols** (CLASS 18, FORM 23, ROLE 7,
  OWN-TEAM 11, BIO 4, FIXTURE 28) + id/meta. PIT, cold-start flagged (`has_class/has_form/has_bio`).
- `data/model_targets.csv` — **2,760 rows**: 17 `y_*` realised components, `recon_pts`,
  `official_pts` (label; 2023 old-scale, 2025/26 modern), `lat_sw/ls/potm/other`, `target_pts`,
  `has_label`, `is_modern`, `team_scrums_won`, `team_lineout_steal`, `min_share`, `potm_winner`.

This plan builds the **model layer** under Strategy C: predict the measurable components → score
them deterministically with the known modern formula → add a forward-chained latent baseline →
rank for selection. Everything lands under a new `model/` package; the extraction stores are
read-only inputs and must not be regenerated by this work.

**Decision update (2026-06-16):** model choice is **baseline-gated, not assumed optimal**. The
data shape is the constraint: the 2025 backtest trains on only 30 fixtures / 1,380 rows, many
players have very few 6N appearances, and the scored components have very different
distributions. The objective is therefore not "make LightGBM win"; it is "find the simplest
component model that beats naive-form on selection value without leaking or overfitting." GLM/Ridge
and naive-form are real contenders, not ceremonial baselines.

`tries, tackles, metres, try_assists, conversion_goals, penalty_goals, drop_goals_converted,

Non-scoring (drop from the points pipeline): `clean_breaks, runs, passes, missed_tackles`.

### Target-shape implications (VERIFIED 2026-06-16)
- High-volume components (`tackles`, `metres`) behave like ordinary rate regression and should
  carry most of the model's effort.
- Medium/sparse components (`tries`, `try_assists`, `defenders_beaten`, `offload`,
  `tackle_turnover`, `penalties_conceded`) are zero-inflated count/rate problems. Model them with
  Poisson/Tweedie or a hurdle-style `P(>0) × expected-count` if that beats the simpler baseline.
- Kicking (`conversion_goals`, `penalty_goals`) is an assignment/gating problem because only the
  likely goal-kicker should receive meaningful mass. Unconditional per-player regression is wrong.
- Extreme rare events (`drop_goals_converted`, `red_cards`, usually `yellow_cards`) are not worth
  flexible modelling on this sample. Use zero/position priors unless out-of-fold evidence says
  otherwise.
- Direct `target_pts` regression is allowed only as a **cross-check baseline**. It blends modern and
  old scoring, cannot use 2024 point labels, and chases the API-blind latent noise. It must not
  replace Strategy C.

### Allowed APIs (cite when used; do not invent)
- **scikit-learn**: `sklearn.model_selection.GroupKFold(n_splits).split(X, y, groups)`;
  `sklearn.linear_model.Ridge` / `ElasticNet`; `sklearn.metrics.mean_absolute_error`;
  `sklearn.preprocessing.StandardScaler`. (Docs: scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/...)
- **LightGBM sklearn API**: `lightgbm.LGBMRegressor(objective=..., n_estimators=, learning_rate=,
  num_leaves=, min_child_samples=, reg_lambda=, random_state=)`; `.fit(X, y, eval_set=[(Xv,yv)],
  callbacks=[lightgbm.early_stopping(50), lightgbm.log_evaluation(0)])`. Native NaN handling (pass
  cold-start NaN straight through). Valid `objective`: `"poisson"`, `"tweedie"`, `"regression_l1"`,
  `"quantile"`, `"regression"`. (Docs: lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/pythonapi/)
- **Anti-patterns**: no `sklearn.model_selection.TimeSeriesSplit` misuse across seasons (use
  GroupKFold on `fixture_id` + explicit season chaining); no `objective="count"` (not a LightGBM
  objective); no deep-learning libs (data far too small); do NOT `fillna(0)` for LightGBM.

## Phase 2 — Splits + fold infrastructure (`model/splits.py`)

**Design (locked):**
- **Holdout: 2026** — sealed. Touched once, in the final phase. Modern scoring.
- **Backtest fold:** train components on **2023+2024**, predict **2025**.
- **Deployment fold:** train components on **2023+2024+2025**, predict **2026**.
- **Hyperparameter tuning:** `GroupKFold(n_splits=5)` grouped on `fixture_id` (all ~46 players in a
  match stay together — prevents match-level leakage) over the *component-training* years only.
- **Within-season prediction is round-by-round PIT already** — `build_features.py` decays FORM/
  CLASS strictly on `date < fixture_date`, so 2025 R3 features legitimately include 2025 R1–R2. The
  component model is therefore trained once on the train years and applied to every test-season row;
  no per-round component retraining needed (features carry the within-season recency).
- **Latent calibration is SEPARATE and forward-chained** (this is the subtle correctness point):
  the train years have **no modern labels** (2023 old-scale, 2024 unlabelled), so `_fit_latent`
  cannot run on them. Instead, when predicting round R of a modern season, fit the latent on **all
  modern-labelled matches with `date < asof_R` (that round's date)**. For 2025 R1 (no prior modern), fall
  back to `potm_base_global`/`other_global` shrink (cold-start). For 2026, fit on all of 2025
  (+ 2026 rounds < R if chaining within 2026).

**What to implement** in `model/splits.py`:
- `component_train(df, test_season)` → boolean mask of rows used to train components (all 6N rows
  with `season < test_season`).
- `group_kfold_indices(df_train, n_splits=5)` → yields `(tr_idx, va_idx)` via GroupKFold on
  `fixture_id`.
- `latent_calib_rows(df, asof_date)` → modern-labelled rows with `date < asof_date`
  (`is_modern & has_label & date < asof_date`).
- `round_iter(df, test_season)` → yields `(round, asof_date, row_idx)` in date order for the test
  season (drives forward-chained latent + eval).

**Verification checklist**
- Backtest: `component_train(df, 2025)` selects exactly the 2023+2024 rows (1,380).
- `latent_calib_rows(df, <2025 R1 date>)` is empty (cold-start) and `len>0` for later rounds.
- No fold's validation `fixture_id` appears in its train split (assert disjoint groups).

**Anti-pattern guards**: never include `test_season` rows in `component_train`; never include rows
with `date >= asof` in `latent_calib_rows` (forward-chain only); 2023 `official_pts` must never
enter latent calibration (it's old-scale — `is_modern` filter handles this, assert it).

## Phase 3 — Baselines (`model/baselines.py`) — the must-beat bar

**What to implement** (predict the 13 scored components, then score with `score_components`):
1. **B0 — naive form**: predicted component rate = `form_per80_<comp>`; multiply by expected
   minutes (Phase 4) /80. For components without a `form_per80_*` (e.g. cards), use the
   position×prior base rate. This is nearly free and is the bar the ML must clear.
2. **B1 — Ridge / ElasticNet** per component on the rate features. Impute cold-start NaN with the
   position-group mean **and add the existing `has_*` flag** (do not 0-impute silently);
   `StandardScaler` the inputs. Linear, won't overfit 1,380 rows — the sanity ceiling.
3. **B2 — GLM count baseline**: Poisson/Tweedie GLM-style regressors where scikit-learn support is
   available (`PoissonRegressor`, `TweedieRegressor`) with strong regularization. This is a serious
   candidate for sparse components, not just a diagnostic.
4. **B3 — direct points cross-check**: a deliberately simple Ridge/ElasticNet model predicting
   modern-labelled `official_pts` for 2025 only (and later 2026 once unlocked). This is for
   comparison and sanity, not the deployable architecture.

**Doc references**: `sklearn.linear_model.Ridge`, `StandardScaler`, `GroupKFold` (Phase 0 APIs).
FORM rate column names in `build_features.py:53-58` (`form_per80_*`).

**Verification checklist**
- Component baselines produce a `target_pts_hat` for every 2025 row; assemble + score runs end-to-end.
- Report points MAE vs `official_pts` on 2025 for B0/B1/B2 and direct-points B3.
- Report value-of-picked-XV, captain hit-rate, and within-position Spearman for every baseline.

**Anti-pattern guards**: do not regress directly on `target_pts` here (predict components → score).
Do not let Ridge/GLM see NaN (they error) — impute + flag. B3 must never be used as the final
model unless Strategy C catastrophically fails and that failure is documented.

## Phase 4 — Component model + minutes (`model/train_components.py`)

**What to implement**
1. **Expected minutes** (points scale with time):
   - Pre-team-sheet: start with `form_minutes_recent × form_start_rate` plus position/roster priors;
     use a small Ridge/ElasticNet or tiny LightGBM only if it beats that prior out-of-fold.
   - Post-team-sheet: use known `started`, `jersey`, and position as inputs; predict `minutes_hat`.
   - All component models predict **per-80 rate**, then convert via `× minutes_hat / 80`.
2. **Per-component objective map** (train on realised per-80 rate where `minutes >= 10`; predict
   non-negative per-match outputs after multiplying by `minutes_hat / 80`):
   - `tackles`: plain L2/Ridge or Poisson; LightGBM only if it improves OoF and 2025.
   - `metres`: Tweedie (`tweedie_variance_power≈1.3`) or `regression_l1`; this is a prime LightGBM
     candidate because fixture/opponent interactions matter.
   - `tries`, `try_assists`, `defenders_beaten`, `offload`, `tackle_turnover`,
     `penalties_conceded`: Poisson or hurdle-style `P(>0) × expected-count`; use tiny LightGBM only
     when it beats B0/B1/B2 out-of-fold.
   - `conversion_goals`, `penalty_goals`: gate by likely kicker (`role_goal_kicker_rate`, team,
     jersey/position when post-team-sheet) then model team kicking total × assignment probability.
   - `drop_goals_converted`, `red_cards`: default to 0 or a tiny position prior. Do not train a
     flexible model unless future data shows enough nonzero events.
   - `yellow_cards`: position/discipline base rate; a small Poisson only if it improves calibration.
3. **Candidate engines per component**:
   - B0/B1/B2 from Phase 3 remain eligible final models.
   - LightGBM is the interaction capturer, not the default winner. Use it for components where
     `opp_*`, `wr_*`, role, and cold-start interactions add measurable value.
   - A simple component blend is allowed: average GLM/Ridge and tiny LightGBM predictions **only for
     components where both beat naive-form out-of-fold**. Do not blend weak learners for appearance.
4. **Conservative LightGBM search space** (small data, 30 fixtures in the 2025 backtest):
   - `num_leaves ∈ {7, 15, 31}`
   - `min_child_samples ∈ {30, 50, 80, 120}`
   - `learning_rate ∈ {0.02, 0.05}`
   - `n_estimators ∈ {500, 1000, 2000}` with early stopping
   - `reg_lambda ∈ {1, 5, 10, 25}`
   - `feature_fraction ∈ {0.6, 0.8, 1.0}` and `bagging_fraction ∈ {0.7, 0.9, 1.0}` if available in
     the sklearn API used
5. Tune via Phase-2 GroupKFold with `early_stopping(50)` on held-out fixture groups. Select by a
   combined score: component OoF error for high-value components plus 2025 assembled
   value-of-picked-XV. Prefer the simpler model on ties.

**Doc references**: LightGBM `LGBMRegressor` signature + `early_stopping`/`log_evaluation` callbacks
(Phase 0). `role_goal_kicker_rate` source in `build_features.py:152-156`.

**Verification checklist**
- Each component model trains and predicts on 2025 with no error; predicted per-80 rates are
  non-negative; `metres` predictions in a sane range (~0–150).
- Component-level MAE (per-80) beats B0 naive-form per-80 for the high-value components
  (tries, metres, tackles) on GroupKFold.
- Kicking predictions concentrate on goal-kickers (assert non-kickers' predicted kicking ≈0).
- Rare-event components do not move aggregate points materially unless they prove out-of-fold value.
- A per-component model registry records which engine won (`naive`, `ridge`, `glm`, `lgbm`, `blend`,
  `zero_prior`) and why.

**Anti-pattern guards**: no `fillna(0)` before LightGBM (kills the cold-start signal — native NaN);
do not train on rows from the test season; `objective` must be one of the valid strings (Phase 0);
do not predict the 4 non-scoring components for the points path; do not promote LightGBM for a
component that fails the baseline gate.

## Phase 5 — Assemble points + forward-chained latent (`model/assemble.py`)

**What to implement**
1. Build the predicted component dict per row from Phase-4 outputs; call
   `score_components(c_hat, is_forward)` → `recon_pts_hat`. **Reuse the function**, do not re-code.
2. For each test round R (Phase-2 `round_iter`): fit the latent via `_fit_latent` on
   `latent_calib_rows(df, asof_R)` (forward-chained modern subset). Apply `a·sw_drive + b·ls_drive +
   lat_potm_baseline + other_const` to round-R rows. SW/LS drives at inference use **expected** team
   set-piece (the `ownteam_*`/`opp_concede_*` features) × role × `minutes_hat/80` — NOT realised
   team counts (those are label-only). POTM at inference = `15 × P(POTM)` from `role_potm_rate` +
   predicted-points rank within the match (a simple within-match softmax/rank → prob).
3. `target_pts_hat = recon_pts_hat + latent_hat`.
4. Optional selection-rank head: train a LightGBM `lambdarank`/ranking model as a comparison, grouped
   by round+position bucket (or match+position as a diagnostic). It may improve ordering even if its
   point MAE is worse. It is a selection overlay, not a replacement for calibrated point forecasts.

**Doc references**: `_fit_latent` (build_targets.py:186) return keys; `sw_drive/ls_drive` definition
(build_targets.py — `is_forward·team_scrums_won·min_share`; at inference swap realised team count
for the PIT `ownteam_scrums_won` estimate). `opp_concede_lineout_steal` feature for LS tilt.

**Verification checklist**
- `target_pts_hat` produced for all 2025 (backtest) and 2026 (deployment) rows, no NaN.
- Latent at 2025 R1 uses the cold-start fallback (assert `latent_calib_rows` empty there).
- Forward-chain assertion: the latent fit for round R used zero rows dated ≥ R.
- Ranking-head picks are compared against point-model picks using the same XV constraints.

**Anti-pattern guards**: inference latent must use **expected** (PIT feature) set-piece, never the
realised `team_scrums_won`/`team_lineout_steal`/`potm_winner` columns (label-only leakage); latent
calibration never pools 2026 into a 2025 prediction (chain strictly by date). Ranking features use
the same leakage denylist as point models.

## Phase 6 — Evaluation (`model/evaluate.py`) + run harness (`model/run.py`)

**What to implement** — evaluate on **selection quality, not just MAE**:
1. Points: `mean_absolute_error(official_pts, target_pts_hat)` on modern-labelled rows, by season
   (2025 backtest, 2026 deployment), by round, by position.
2. Selection metrics (the real objective):
   - top-N overlap: of the model's top-N predicted, how many are in the actual top-N scorers (per
     round, per position bucket).
   - captain hit-rate: model's #1 pick = actual #1 (or top-3).
   - **value-of-picked-XV vs hindsight-optimal XV** (sum of actual points of the model's chosen XV ÷
     best-possible XV that round), respecting positional constraints.
   - Spearman rank corr of `target_pts_hat` vs `official_pts` within position.
3. `model/run.py` orchestrates: backtest (train 23+24 → 2025) and deployment (train 23+24+25 →
   2026), printing a comparison table **final component ensemble vs LightGBM-only vs GLM/Ridge vs
   naive-form vs direct-points cross-check vs optional rank head** across all metrics.

**Verification checklist**
- The final promoted model beats B0 and B1 on points MAE **and** on value-of-picked-XV for 2025; if
  it doesn't beat naive-form, stop and investigate (the engineered features may already be the
  signal).
- The final promoted model beats B0 on value-of-picked-XV and does not materially worsen MAE or
  position-bucket rank correlation. A lower-MAE model that picks worse XVs is not the winner.
- 2026 is evaluated exactly once, after 2025 results are accepted.
- Per-round table shows no degenerate rounds (e.g. all-zero predictions).

**Anti-pattern guards**: do not tune anything on 2026; do not report only MAE (selection is the
goal); do not silently drop unlabelled rows from selection eval — pick from the full roster, score
only labelled picks.

## Phase 7 — Generalisation failure fallbacks

These fallbacks are part of the design, not a sign the work failed. The training set is small enough
that "simpler wins" is a plausible and acceptable outcome.

**Failure triggers**
- The promoted model fails to beat B0 naive-form on 2025 value-of-picked-XV.
- LightGBM improves OoF component error but worsens 2025 selection value or shows unstable per-round
  rankings.
- 2026, when unlocked once, materially underperforms 2025 backtest in both MAE and selection value.
- Component predictions become degenerate (all-zero, implausible metres/tackles, or kicking mass on
  non-kickers).

**Fallback ladder**
1. **Component-level demotion:** for each weak component, fall back to the best of B0/B1/B2 or a
   zero/position prior. Keep stronger models only for components that generalise.
2. **LightGBM simplification:** raise `min_child_samples`, reduce `num_leaves`, increase
   `reg_lambda`, and remove low-value feature families one at a time. Prefer stable rank metrics
   over small MAE wins.
3. **Blend shrinkage:** if GBT is noisy but sometimes useful, blend `0.25 × GBT + 0.75 × GLM/Ridge`
   or shrink GBT predictions toward the naive-form rate. Tune blend weight on fixture-grouped OoF
   only.
4. **Latent shrinkage:** if forward-chained latent calibration is unstable early in a season, shrink
   `a`, `b`, POTM baseline, and `other_const` toward global/position means by calibration sample
   size. For 2025 R1 use only global/position priors.
5. **Mode separation:** if pre-team-sheet performance is poor but post-team-sheet is good, ship only
   the post-team-sheet picker and label the pre-team-sheet output as provisional.
6. **Direct-points escape hatch:** if Strategy C cannot beat direct-points Ridge on 2025 selection
   value after leakage checks, keep Strategy C for explanation but use direct-points Ridge as a
   temporary production baseline. Document that it cannot exploit 2024 labels and should be
   revisited as more modern seasons arrive.
7. **Human-readable fallback output:** if no learned model beats B0, deploy naive-form + fixture
   difficulty + role priors as the picker. This is still a valid model and may be the honest winner
   with 30 matches of training data.

**Post-fallback requirement:** after any fallback, rerun the same 2025 backtest table and record
which components/models were demoted. Do not touch 2026 to choose a fallback.

### Promoted model — component-ensemble (registry-gated)
The headline model is the per-component baseline-gated ensemble. LightGBM is promoted for a
component only when it beats the best simple baseline on **training-fold (2023+2024) GroupKFold
OOF MAE by ≥2% relative** (`LGBM_MARGIN = 0.02`). Selection was done on the backtest train years
only — no 2025/2026 peek. Final registry (`data/model_component_registry.csv`):

| component | kind | engine | reason |
|---|---|---|---|

### `cdx` Experiment 2 — selector score for XV picking
The second loop introduced `model/research.py` and a ledgered autoresearch pass over selection
knobs. The accepted candidate keeps calibrated point predictions unchanged, but adds a separate

`sel_score = zscore(target_pts_hat) + 0.25 * zscore(rank_score)`

`rank_score` comes from the existing LightGBM ranking head; `target_pts_hat` remains the calibrated
point forecast used for MAE. The accepted 0.25 tilt improved the 2025 dev XV value in 3 of 5
rounds; a stronger 0.5 tilt was rejected because the gain was too round-fragile.

Artifacts: `research/LEDGER.md`, `research/best_config.json`, `research/sealed_2026.json`, and

### Notes / caveats
- **Captain hit-rate is noisy** (5 rounds/season): top-1 is 0.000 for the point models in 2025
  and only the rank head / naive land occasional hits in 2026. Judge selection quality on
  `value_xv` and `top15`, not captain hit-rate, at this sample size.
- **Pre-team-sheet view is provisional** (fallback step 5): without `started`/`jersey`, MAE
  degrades to ~8.6–8.9 and value_xv drops, because minutes become much harder to forecast. The
  headline numbers assume the team sheet is known. Treat pre-team-sheet ranking as a weaker,
  earlier-in-the-week view.
- **Latent LS coefficient (`b`) is unstable** across forward-chained rounds (≈0 → 1.68 → 1.58 →
  0.01). This is immaterial to selection: lineout-steal latent contributes <1 pt even to jumpers,
  and `value_xv`/MAE are unaffected. If it ever moves the ranking, the documented remedy is the
  shrinkage fallback (fallback step 4 — shrink `a`/`b` toward 0); it was not needed here.
